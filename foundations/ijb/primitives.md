# The Six Primitives

Quick reference for the foundational elements of It's Just Business.

## Overview

All business reality can be described using six primitives. These are never visual artifacts themselves - they are facts that get projected into spatial representations.

## The Primitives

### 1. Things

**What they are:** Objects that exist

**Examples:**
- Person
- System
- Dataset
- Building
- Document
- Team
- Product

**In visualization:** Become objects in the scene

**Key principle:** Visual form indicates existence, not importance or authority

---

### 2. Scopes

**What they are:** Contexts in which things exist

**Examples:**
- Internal vs external
- Team boundaries
- Environments (dev, staging, prod)
- Geographic regions
- Time periods
- Regulatory jurisdictions

**In visualization:** Become planes, layers, color fields, or contextual backdrops

**Key principle:** Things can appear in multiple scopes without duplication. Scopes never trap things.

---

### 3. Paths

**What they are:** Connections along which things move

**Properties:**
- Directional
- Time-sensitive
- Can be congested
- Can fail

**Examples:**
- Data flows
- Approval chains
- Certification pipelines
- Communication channels
- Delivery routes

**In visualization:** Become routed lines, corridors, flows, or tracks

**Key principle:** If a line is drawn, something must move along it. No decorative edges.

---

### 4. Observed

**What they are:** Facts that were witnessed

**Examples:**
- Event logged
- Metric recorded
- Inspection completed
- Observation made late
- Observation absent

**In visualization:** Become highlights, pulses, heat, markers, timestamps, or badges

**Key principle:** Observation changes how things appear, not what they are.

---

### 5. Constraints

**What they are:** Limits that restrict movement

**Examples:**
- Budget caps
- Regulatory requirements
- Access controls
- Capacity limits
- Policy restrictions
- Technical limitations

**In visualization:** Become blocked paths, gated interactions, conditional traversal, or annotated limits

**Key principle:** Constraints restrict movement, not existence.

---

### 6. Time

**What it is:** The dimension that orders everything

**Controls:**
- Appearance
- Disappearance
- State change
- Ordering
- Traversal eligibility

**Examples:**
- Scrubbing through history
- Stepping through events
- Freezing state for inspection
- Observing lag and delay

**In visualization:** The only dimension that truly moves

**Key principle:** Nothing exists "outside" time in the visualization.

---

## The Critical Separation

### Descriptive Layer (Never Visual)
- Things exist
- They are in scopes
- Paths connect them
- Observations occurred
- Constraints applied
- Time ordered everything

This layer is queried, never drawn.

### Spatial Projection (The Visualization)
- Projects the same facts into space
- No new information introduced
- Multiple projections possible from same facts

---

## Reality Check Question

When someone points at the visualization and asks "what is that?", the answer should be one of these six primitives.

If the answer is "strategy", "culture", "alignment", or "risk posture", it has failed.
