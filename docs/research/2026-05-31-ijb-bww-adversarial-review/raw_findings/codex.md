## Errors I Caught

- The analysis overstates D1 as "no participant/attribute slot in `path-call`." `path` has fixed relation fields: `within=` and `moves=` in both path forms (`foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:61-62`), and `moves=` has a document vocabulary rule (`foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:123`). The valid criticism is absence of open-ended relation attributes and n-ary participants.
- The analysis's original `constraint` -> state law label is not byte-faithful. The primitive definition says "Limits that restrict movement" (`foundations/ijb/primitives.md:90`) and "Constraints restrict movement, not existence" (`foundations/ijb/primitives.md:102`).
- `observed` -> Event is too broad. The grammar says `observed(asserts=...)` points to "an existing assertion" (`foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:117`), while event-bearing validity is enforced on `time(event=...)` (`foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:93` and `:118-119`).
- The counterfactual citation is sloppy. `faq.md:66-70` is about uncertainty, not counterfactuals. The closest byte evidence is `faq.md:72-78`, which forbids projecting hypothetical futures while allowing facts about plans.
- The M6 `time` claim is directionally right but too flat. `time` is not merely an ordering index in the primitive prose: it controls appearance, disappearance, state change, ordering, and traversal eligibility (`foundations/ijb/primitives.md:110-115`).

## Things The Analysis Missed

- `scope` has its own overload risk after the Class/Kind mapping is withdrawn. The primitive examples include team boundaries, environments, geographic regions, time periods, and regulatory jurisdictions (`foundations/ijb/primitives.md:35-40`). The DAG-TOML mapping also classifies `[meta].framework_profile` and `[meta].template_kind` as `scope/structural` (`spec.md:635-636`). That makes `scope` do contextual, organizational, spatial, temporal, jurisdictional, and meta-schema work.
- The free-text deviation is a separate overload/redundancy surface. DAG-TOML intentionally breaks IJB's canonical grammar prose restriction (`spec.md:677-693`), defaults prose to `observed` (`spec.md:697-700`), and gives normative prose an additional `constraint/policy` role (`spec.md:702-708`). One field surface can therefore carry both observed and constraint roles.
- The validator is not semantic evidence for BWW adequacy. It explicitly enforces the structural surface (`validators/validate_ijb_conformance.py:5-16`) and declares free-text forbidden-concept matching and cross-document structural-instance pairing out of scope (`validators/validate_ijb_conformance.py:38-44`; `spec.md:743-749`).
- §12 introduces cryptographic/document-closure machinery that is explicitly primitive-agnostic: `spec.md:1021-1025` says §12 is "envelope-agnostic and primitive-agnostic." It may be necessary for assurance, but it is not evidence that the six business-reality primitives are BWW-complete.

## Overall Verdict

The overall claim is sound: IJB looks usable as a constrained core, but the absolute "all business reality can be described with six primitives" claim is not defensible by the BWW standard. My highest-confidence reason is the grammar-level binary `path` shape: both path forms have exactly one `from=` and one `to=` (`foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:60-62`), and no other primitive supplies a general n-ary relation/objectification mechanism. Even after correcting the overstatement about path attributes, that is a hard construct deficit for BWW-style representation of mutual/emergent properties and relation instances.
