# Codex Findings - IJB Primitive #1 Naming

## 1. Per-axis verdict

1. **Official meaning of primitive #1: `party`**. Evidence: `foundations/ijb/primitives.md:11-14` defines primitive #1 as Things, "Objects that exist"; `foundations/ijb/primitives.md:26` says visual form indicates existence, not importance or authority; replay emits existence claims at `foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:97-101`; `spec.md:573-589` makes the six current primitives, including Thing, normative IJB substrate. The `referent` draft itself concedes the semantic shift at `/srv/repos/external/kasselman/ijb-primitive-naming-review/07-drafts-for-referent.md:108-116`; WordNet/Farlex sense 2 is real, but it reframes the primitive from existence anchoring to reference anchoring, which is too close to the category shift that sank `matter`.

2. **Internal collision: `party`**. Re-run whole-word scan against upstream, excluding this live review directory, returned `referent=0`, `referents=0`, `reference=828`, `references=391`, `referenced=122`, `party=111`, `parties=43`. Evidence of reference-family saturation is direct: identifier reference rules and dereferencing are already canonical grammar at `foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:28-32` and `foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:110-124`; `--check-references-exist` is a validator mode in `AGENTS.md:62-66`; `reference/database/` is an established non-normative surface in `AGENTS.md:216-217`. `party` has no schema role in a targeted scan for `[[parties]]`, `party_id`, `party_kind`, `NoSuchParty`, or validator/type names; its loaded uses are ordinary English such as `profiles/agent-assurance/tiers/enterprise.toml:63`.

3. **Legal / regulatory risk: `referent`**. Evidence: GDPR Article 4 defines `data subject` and `third party` distinctly, so neither candidate is a GDPR hard blocker; the local repo already has legal/assurance `party` surfaces at `profiles/agent-assurance/tiers/enterprise.toml:63`, `docs/research/2026-05-22-spec-foundations-research/prompts/05-follow-up-exa-deep-researchers.md:67`, and `docs/research/2026-05-22-spec-foundations-research/follow-up/gemini-streams-a-b-c-d.md:70`. `referent` is externally lexical/semiotic rather than legal; Merriam-Webster gives "one that refers or is referred to" and The Free Dictionary/Farlex includes the proposition-term sense, so audit-misinterpretation risk is lower for `referent` on this axis alone. Sources checked: https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/referent, https://www.thefreedictionary.com/referent, https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/, https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63c.html.

4. **Brand / tone: `party`**. Evidence: IJB presents itself as "business reality ... without abstraction" at `foundations/ijb/README.md:1-10`; it lists primitive #1 plainly as "Things - Objects that exist" at `foundations/ijb/README.md:14-23`; `foundations/ijb/why-this-matters.md:47-64` requires factual grounding and resistance to abstraction; `foundations/ijb/faq.md:13-15` says the framework has six primitives and refuses to abstract. The `referent` draft acknowledges the cost at `/srv/repos/external/kasselman/ijb-primitive-naming-review/07-drafts-for-referent.md:257-260`. This axis is decisive because primitive names are the first user-facing ontology, not hidden implementation detail.

5. **Replay and ergonomics: `party`**. Evidence: current replay is literal and existence-based at `foundations/ijb/canonical-assertion-grammar.md:97-107`; both drafts recommend dropping the noun in high-frequency replay at `/srv/repos/external/kasselman/ijb-primitive-naming-review/06-drafts-for-party.md:39-64` and `/srv/repos/external/kasselman/ijb-primitive-naming-review/07-drafts-for-referent.md:129-153`. With replay equalized, the live difference is keys/types/errors/sections; `party`, despite participation baggage, is more legible in `party=`, `[[parties]]`, and `NoSuchPartyError` than `referent=`, `[[referents]]`, and `NoSuchReferentError`, especially given the reference-family saturation above.

6. **Directional ambiguity: `party`**. Evidence: the `referent` draft states the needed restriction at `/srv/repos/external/kasselman/ijb-primitive-naming-review/07-drafts-for-referent.md:201-204`: English can mean either "one that refers" or "one that is referred to", but IJB would use only the passive sense. Merriam-Webster confirms the bidirectionality. In a precision-first framework, a primitive whose gloss includes its converse is a precision liability.

## 3. Terminal recommendation

`adopt_party`

Concrete reason: `referent`'s cost is disqualifying because it simultaneously reframes the first primitive away from existence, collides by proximity with the repo's saturated reference/resolution vocabulary, violates IJB's plain anti-abstraction brand, and requires a passive-only carve-out for a bidirectional word. `party` has real participation and legal-register baggage, but the inspected bytes show those are ordinary-English collisions, not current schema-role collisions, and they are easier to fence in a SPEC note than `referent`'s foundation-level semantic shift.

## 4. The decisive factor

The decisive factor is that the first IJB primitive is defined and replayed as existence, while `referent` would make that foundation sound like another reference-resolution concept in a repo already saturated with reference machinery.
